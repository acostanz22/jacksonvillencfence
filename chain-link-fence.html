Chain-link fencing

Chain-link fence installation in Jacksonville, NC

Chain link for residential yards, dog runs, and commercial perimeters. Galvanized is the basic fabric. Vinyl-coated fabric is the better spec where salt air reaches the lot.

When this fence is the right job

Use chain link when you need a boundary, a dog run, or a secure lot and you do not need a solid screen. It is the wrong product if the goal is to block the neighbor’s view, unless a windscreen is added and the frame is built for that wind load. Commercial fabric and terminal posts are heavier than a backyard kit.

How the work goes

  1. 1Mark the line and the openings. Corners, ends, and gates are laid out before any post is set.
  2. 2Set terminal posts. End, corner, and gate posts go in concrete. They carry the tension.
  3. 3Set line posts and rail. The framework goes in, then the fabric is stretched and tied.
  4. 4Hang the gates. Gates sit on posts sized for the opening, not on a light line post.
  5. 5Add coating or screen if it is part of the job. Vinyl-coated fabric or a privacy screen is decided up front, not bolted on a frame that cannot hold it.

Residential chain link is a common side-yard and dog fence in Jacksonville. Closer to Sneads Ferry, Swansboro, and the New River, salt air shortens the life of bare steel, so a vinyl-coated fabric is worth specifying. Inland, toward Richlands, galvanized is usually enough. A privacy screen turns the fence into a sail. That is a commercial-grade conversation, not a slat pack on a light frame.

What changes the estimate

We price the line on your lot. These are the pieces that move the number.

Height and gauge

A 4-foot residential run and a taller commercial fabric are different steel.

Coating

Galvanized and vinyl-coated fabric do not cost the same, and the coast pushes the choice.

Framework

Terminal posts, rail, and tension wire are what keep the fabric from sagging.

Gates

A walk gate is simple. A double drive gate needs posts that can carry it.

Screen

Privacy slats or a windscreen add material and a wind load the posts must hold.

Ground

Soft sand, roots, and a slope change post depth and how the fabric is stretched.

Questions about chain-link fencing

Is chain link only for commercial lots?

No. It is a common residential fence for dogs, side yards, and people who want a boundary without a solid wall. Commercial work uses a heavier framework, taller fabric, and gates sized for trucks.

Can you add privacy to chain link?

A screen can block the view. It also catches wind, so the posts and rails have to be up to that load. A screen on a light residential frame is a different job than a screen on a commercial line.

Which coating holds up near the coast?

Bare galvanized is the basic product. Closer to Sneads Ferry, Swansboro, and the river, vinyl-coated fabric resists corrosion longer. Inland Richlands and much of Jacksonville can use galvanized when salt exposure is low.

How is a chain-link install sequenced?

We mark the line, set terminal posts in concrete, add line posts and rail, then stretch the fabric and tie it off. Gates hang on posts sized for the opening.

Can chain link sit on the property line?

In Jacksonville, a fence may sit on the line if it stays out of easements, sidewalks, and the right-of-way. In unincorporated Onslow County a zoning permit is not required and the ordinance does not set a fence location. The line still has to be yours. A survey beats a guess.

Talk through the line

Call or send the town, the approximate length, and the gate count. We will tell you if the job is a repair, a replacement, or a different material than you started with.

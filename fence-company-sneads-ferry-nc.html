What fence work looks like here

Vinyl-coated chain link, vinyl privacy, and aluminum are the specs that avoid a rust problem you would see sooner here than in Richlands. We do not set posts in standing water and hope. If a corner of the lot holds water, we say so and move the plan, or we wait for drier ground. Year-round houses and houses that sit empty part of the year both need a gate that latches without someone on site to adjust it every week.

Salt first

Coated chain link, vinyl, and aluminum are the default conversation this close to the inlet.

Soft ground

A post in saturated sand will move. We check that before concrete goes in the hole.

Empty-house gates

Gates are hung and latched so the fence still works when you are not there to lift a sagging leaf.

Will a galvanized chain-link fence rust in Sneads Ferry?

Bare galvanized steel ages faster next to the inlet. Vinyl-coated fabric is the chain-link spec we recommend on these lots.

Can you set posts in a yard that floods?

Not in standing water. We look at the low spots first. A line can often move a few feet onto firmer ground. If it cannot, we wait or we change the plan.

Is a solid privacy fence a problem in the wind here?

A solid fence catches wind off open water. Post spacing has to match that. On a very exposed edge, a style that lets some air through can be the sturdier build.
